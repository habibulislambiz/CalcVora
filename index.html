CGPA to Percentage: The Complete Guide

CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average) and percentage both measure the same academic performance, but the conversion system is not uniform across Indian universities. This guide breaks down every major university's official formula so you can convert your CGPA accurately.

When you fill out a job application, a higher-studies admission form, or a scholarship application, you are often asked for a percentage. And that is where the most common mistake happens: every university uses a different formula.

Using the wrong formula can create a mismatch between your application form and your degree certificate — a problem that can delay admissions, loan approvals, or job offers.

What is the difference between CGPA and percentage?

CGPA

CGPA is the average of your overall academic performance on a 10-point scale (sometimes 7-point or 4-point). It is calculated by multiplying each subject's grade points by its credits, adding them up, and dividing by the total credits.

%

Percentage expresses the same performance on a 100-point scale. The Indian job market, government exams (SSC, UPSC, banking), and many postgraduate programs still use percentage-based eligibility criteria, which is why CGPA has to be converted.

⚠️ Important: CBSE's 9.5 multiplier and VTU's (CGPA − 0.75) × 10 produce very different results. The same 8.0 CGPA becomes 76% under CBSE, 72.5% under VTU's old scheme, and 80% under Anna University. Using the wrong formula can create a serious mismatch.

University-wise CGPA to Percentage Formulas

Below are the official formulas for India's most common boards and universities. Each formula comes with a worked example so you can verify your own calculation.

1

CBSE / Delhi University

CBSE introduced its grading system in 2010, and this formula has been the standard ever since. CBSE analyzed five years of result data and found that students scoring 91–100 marks (A1 grade, 10 CGPA) averaged around 95%. Hence, 95 ÷ 10 = 9.5 became the multiplier.

Percentage = CGPA × 9.5

Example: CGPA 8.5 → 8.5 × 9.5 = 80.75%

Note: CBSE itself states this is an indicative (approximate) percentage, not exact marks. Delhi University and several central universities use the same formula.

2

VTU (Visvesvaraya Technological University)

VTU's formula depends on your scheme. This inconsistency is a common source of confusion, so check your admission year carefully.

Old scheme (2015, 2017, 2018)

(CGPA − 0.75) × 10

CGPA 8.0 → 72.5%

New scheme (2021 onward)

CGPA × 10

CGPA 8.0 → 80%

The same CGPA gives a 7.5% difference between the old and new schemes. Always confirm your scheme before converting.

3

Anna University

Anna University uses a simple multiplier: CGPA multiplied by 10. Keep in mind that grading here is strict — an 'O' grade (10 points) usually requires 90+ marks.

Percentage = CGPA × 10

Example: CGPA 8.4 → 8.4 × 10 = 84%

4

Mumbai University

Mumbai University uses different formulas for different faculties, which is one of the most common conversion mistakes.

Engineering

(7.1 × CGPI) + 11

CGPI 8.0 → 67.8%

Other faculties

(CGPI − 0.75) × 10

CGPI 8.0 → 72.5%

The same CGPI produces a 4.7% difference. Engineering students must use the engineering-specific formula.

5

AKTU (Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam Technical University)

Percentage = CGPA × 10 − 7.5

Example: CGPA 8.0 → (8.0 × 10) − 7.5 = 72.5%

6

GTU / JNTUH / KTU

Percentage = (CGPA − 0.5) × 10

Example: CGPA 8.0 → (8.0 − 0.5) × 10 = 75%

How to Convert Percentage Back to CGPA

If you have a percentage and need a CGPA, reverse the CBSE formula:

CGPA = Percentage ÷ 9.5

Example: 76% → 76 ÷ 9.5 = 8.0 CGPA

⚠️ Note: This reverse formula is only an approximation. If your university uses a different official formula (such as VTU or Mumbai), reverse-calculate using that formula. For job applications, always use the CGPA printed on your original mark sheet rather than a reverse-calculated value.

What Is a Conversion Certificate, and When Do You Need One?

Many universities (especially VTU and Mumbai) issue a Conversion Certificate, also called a Percentage Equivalence Letter. It officially states what your CGPA converts to as a percentage and which formula was used.

When you need it:

  • When a target university or employer does not accept your calculated formula
  • For education loan applications, since many banks require it
  • For government job forms that say "percentage as per university norms"
  • For visa applications or foreign university admissions

Apply through your university's examination section. Processing usually takes 3–7 working days and involves a nominal fee.

Common CGPA Conversion Mistakes to Avoid

Avoiding these five mistakes reduces the risk of your application being rejected:

1

Using ×9.5 for every university

The 9.5 multiplier applies only to CBSE and Delhi University. VTU, Anna, Mumbai, and AKTU each have their own formulas.

2

Ignoring VTU's old vs new scheme

The 2015/2017/2018 batch uses (CGPA − 0.75) × 10, while 2021 onward uses CGPA × 10. Confirm your admission year first.

3

Missing the faculty rule at Mumbai University

Engineering and other faculties use different formulas. The same CGPI can differ by 4.7% depending on which one you use.

4

Confusing CGPA with percentage

A CGPA of 8.0 and 8.0% are completely different values. Always double-check the field label on any form.

5

Rounding off incorrectly

If your result is 72.55%, enter 72.55 on the form — do not round it to 73. Many institutions require you to enter the exact decimal.

Frequently Asked Questions

The seven questions students ask most about CGPA to percentage conversion.

1. Why does CBSE use a 9.5 multiplier? +
CBSE analyzed five years of result data and found that students scoring 91–100 marks (A1 grade, 10 CGPA) averaged around 95%. Since 95 ÷ 10 = 9.5, the board adopted 9.5 as the multiplier. It is an indicative value, not an exact marks conversion.
2. Is CGPA to percentage conversion exact? +
No. CBSE itself describes the conversion as indicative (approximate). Every university uses its own multiplier, so always confirm your institution's official formula before submitting it on any form.
3. What is Mumbai University's formula? +
Engineering students use (7.1 × CGPI) + 11. Students in other faculties use (CGPI − 0.75) × 10. The two formulas produce noticeably different results, so confirm your faculty before converting.
4. What is 8 CGPA in percentage at VTU? +
Under the old scheme (2015/2017/2018): (8.0 − 0.75) × 10 = 72.5%. Under the new scheme (2021 onward): 8.0 × 10 = 80%. The 7.5% difference is significant, so check your admission year.
5. How do I convert percentage back to CGPA? +
Reverse the CBSE formula: CGPA = Percentage ÷ 9.5. For example, 76% ÷ 9.5 = 8.0 CGPA. Keep in mind this is only accurate if your university follows the CBSE formula; VTU, Mumbai, and others require their own reverse calculations.
6. Do all universities use the same formula? +
No. CBSE uses ×9.5, VTU old scheme uses (CGPA − 0.75) × 10, VTU new scheme uses ×10, Anna University uses ×10, Mumbai Engineering uses 7.1 × CGPI + 11, and AKTU uses CGPA × 10 − 7.5. Always use your own university's formula.
7. Does a lower CGPA always mean a lower percentage? +
CGPA and percentage are proportional, so a lower CGPA always yields a lower percentage. However, the multiplier varies by university, so the same CGPA can produce results that differ by 5–7 marks across institutions.
